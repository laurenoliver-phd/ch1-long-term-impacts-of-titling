# Chapter 1: Long-Term Impacts of Land Titling: Evidence from Benin
The following repository contains a description, summary, and instructions for replicating the research that informed the first chapter of my PhD dissertation. The chapter assesses the long-term impacts of the nationwide land titling program that took place in Benin from 2010-2015, known as the Plan Foncier Rural.

## Abstract

Land formalization has received substantial development funding based on the premise that secure property rights can increase investment, agricultural productivity, and household welfare. Yet despite decades of research, evidence that these benefits are large enough to justify the substantial costs of reform remains mixed. This study examines the long-run effects of the *Plan Foncier Rural* (PFR), a randomized land formalization program implemented in rural Benin in 2010–2011. Earlier evaluations identified modest but promising changes in agricultural investment, but no corresponding improvements in productivity or welfare. I combine original treatment assignment data with nationally representative household surveys collected seven and ten years after implementation to test whether these early responses ultimately translated into meaningful economic gains. I find little evidence of long-run improvements in agricultural output, revenue, or household consumption. However, treatment is associated with persistent changes in perceptions of formal and informal authorities, while exploratory heterogeneity analyses suggest that behavioral responses varied with pre-existing social and institutional conditions. Although statistical power limits the ability to rule out modest effects, the results provide little evidence that formalization generated economic gains commensurate with its substantial costs, raising questions about continued large-scale investment in similar reforms across Sub-Saharan Africa.
